# System Usability Scale (SUS)

**Source:** Brooke, J. (1996). SUS: A "quick and dirty" usability scale. In P. W. Jordan et al. (Eds.), *Usability Evaluation in Industry* (pp. 189–194). Taylor & Francis. Free to use with acknowledgement.

**When:** after each world (Plain, Maze, House), after the NASA-TLX. **Time:** about 2 minutes.

**Two adaptations**, both common. Mention them in the paper's methods:
- "this system" is replaced by **"this navigation method"**, so the same wording fits ROVR (voice) and Manual (controller).
- Item 8 adds "(awkward)" after "cumbersome", because non-native English speakers often misread "cumbersome" (Finstad, 2006, *Journal of Usability Studies, 1*(4); Bangor, Kortum & Miller, 2008, *IJHCI, 24*(6)).

---

## Form text

**Participant code:** ______ (filled in by the researcher)
**World:** ○ Plain ○ Maze ○ House

**About the navigation method you used**
For each statement, choose how much you agree, thinking about how you moved around in the world you just finished. Answer every item, even if you're unsure: pick the closest answer.

**Building it:** Google Forms → *Multiple-choice grid*, rows = the 10 statements **in this exact order** (scoring depends on it), columns = 1–5 with "Strongly disagree" and "Strongly agree" on the ends, "Require a response in each row" on.

---

## Scoring

1. Odd items (1, 3, 5, 7, 9): **score − 1**.
2. Even items (2, 4, 6, 8, 10): **5 − score**.
3. Add the ten results (0–40) and **multiply by 2.5**. The SUS score runs 0–100.

The SUS score isn't a percentage. As a reference point, the average across many published studies is about 68 (Sauro, 2011, *A Practical Guide to the System Usability Scale*). Bangor, Kortum & Miller (2009, *Journal of Usability Studies, 4*(3)) give adjective labels ("OK", "Good", "Excellent") for score ranges.

Compare ROVR and Manual per world, and overall as the average of the three worlds.
