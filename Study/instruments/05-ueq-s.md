# User Experience Questionnaire, short version (UEQ-S)

**Source:** Schrepp, M., Hinderks, A., & Thomaschewski, J. (2017). Design and evaluation of a short version of the User Experience Questionnaire (UEQ-S). *International Journal of Interactive Multimedia and Artificial Intelligence, 4*(6), 103–108. Free to use; the official materials and an analysis spreadsheet are at [ueq-online.org](https://www.ueq-online.org).

**When:** after each world (Plain, Maze, House), last in each round, after the SUS. **Time:** about 1 minute.

The UEQ-S adds what the SUS doesn't cover: how pleasant and engaging the navigation method felt (hedonic quality), next to how practical it was (pragmatic quality). Use the item wording exactly as below. The UEQ's authors ask that items aren't reworded.

---

## Form text

**Participant code:** ______ (filled in by the researcher)
**World:** ○ Plain ○ Maze ○ House

**Your impression of the navigation method**
Each line has two opposite words. Choose the circle that best describes how moving around felt in the world you just finished. The closer a circle is to a word, the more that word fits. Don't think too long: your first impression counts.

**Building it:** Google Forms → *Multiple-choice grid* doesn't show a word on each side of a row, so use one *Linear scale* question per pair: 1 to 7, with the left word as the label for 1 and the right word as the label for 7. Keep the items in this order, negative word on the left, as in the UEQ-S.

---

## Scoring

1. Recode each answer from 1–7 to **−3 to +3** (subtract 4). Every item has its positive word on the right, so nothing is reversed.
2. **Pragmatic quality** = mean of items 1–4 (supportive, easy, efficient, clear).
3. **Hedonic quality** = mean of items 5–8 (exciting, interesting, inventive, leading edge).
4. **Overall** = mean of all 8 items.

The UEQ handbook reads means above **+0.8** as a positive impression, below **−0.8** as negative, and in between as neutral. The official UEQ-S spreadsheet also compares results with its benchmark data set.

Compare ROVR and Manual per world on pragmatic and hedonic quality separately: voice navigation might score higher on hedonic quality even where it's slower.
