# NASA Task Load Index, raw version (Raw NASA-TLX)

**Source:** Hart, S. G., & Staveland, L. E. (1988). Development of NASA-TLX (Task Load Index): Results of empirical and theoretical research. *Advances in Psychology, 52*, 139–183. The raw (unweighted) version: Hart, S. G. (2006). NASA-Task Load Index (NASA-TLX); 20 years later. *Proceedings of the HFES Annual Meeting, 50*(9), 904–908.

**When:** after each world (Plain, Maze, House), right after the SSQ. **Time:** about 1–2 minutes.

This is the **raw** version (decided 28 Oct): the six ratings only, without the 15 pairwise comparisons of the weighted version. Say so in the paper's methods.

---

## Form text

**Participant code:** ______ (filled in by the researcher)
**World:** ○ Plain ○ Maze ○ House

**About the tasks you just finished**
Think about all the tasks in the world you just finished, using your navigation method (voice or controller). For each question, choose a point on the scale.

1. **Mental demand.** How mentally demanding was the task?
   Very low ⟵ 0 · 5 · 10 · … · 95 · 100 ⟶ Very high

2. **Physical demand.** How physically demanding was the task?
   Very low ⟵ 0 · 5 · 10 · … · 95 · 100 ⟶ Very high

3. **Temporal demand.** How hurried or rushed was the pace of the task?
   Very low ⟵ 0 · 5 · 10 · … · 95 · 100 ⟶ Very high

4. **Performance.** How successful were you in accomplishing what you were asked to do?
   Perfect ⟵ 0 · 5 · 10 · … · 95 · 100 ⟶ Failure

5. **Effort.** How hard did you have to work to accomplish your level of performance?
   Very low ⟵ 0 · 5 · 10 · … · 95 · 100 ⟶ Very high

6. **Frustration.** How insecure, discouraged, irritated, stressed, and annoyed were you?
   Very low ⟵ 0 · 5 · 10 · … · 95 · 100 ⟶ Very high

**Note for participants**, read aloud before the first round: "For performance, the scale is reversed: the left end means you did perfectly, the right end means you failed."

---

## Building it

The original scale has 21 marks: 0 to 100 in steps of 5. Google Forms' linear scale only goes to 10, so pick one of these:

- **Google Forms (recommended):** one *Multiple-choice grid* per question, with one row and 21 columns labelled 0, 5, 10, …, 100. Put the end labels ("Very low" / "Very high", or "Perfect" / "Failure" for item 4) in the question's description. It's wide, so check it on the tablet you'll use.
- **Microsoft Forms, Qualtrics or Typeform:** a 0–100 slider in steps of 5.
- **Paper:** the original line with 20 boxes; participants mark a box boundary.

Use the same format for every participant, in both conditions.

---

## Scoring

- Each item is already 0–100. Don't reverse **Performance**: on this scale a higher number always means more workload.
- **Raw TLX score** = the average of the six ratings (0–100).
- Also keep the six subscale scores. The analysis plan (§7.1.5) compares the NASA-TLX subscales between ROVR and Manual, per world.
