# Think-aloud instructions (Manual group)

**Who:** Manual (controller) participants only, during the practice and all three worlds (§7.1.4). ROVR participants don't think aloud: everything they say is taken as a command.

**Method:** concurrent think-aloud as in Ericsson & Simon (1993, *Protocol Analysis: Verbal Reports as Data*, MIT Press). Participants say what they're thinking as they go. They aren't asked to explain or justify it, which would slow them down and change how they navigate. Reminders are short and neutral.

The think-aloud audio is later compared with the ROVR group's voice commands (§7.1.5). Thinking aloud may still slow the Manual group a little; report this as a limitation.

---

## 1. Instructions (read aloud before the practice)

> "While you move around today, I'd like you to think aloud. That means saying out loud whatever you're thinking as you go: what you're looking at, where you're trying to go, what you're about to do, and anything that surprises or confuses you. Talk as if you're alone in the room talking to yourself.
> You don't need to explain or make full sentences, and there are no right or wrong things to say. I won't answer or talk back while you're doing the tasks, so don't worry if I'm quiet. If you go silent for a while, I'll just say 'please keep talking'."

## 2. Warm-up (1–2 min, before the headset goes on)

> "Let's practise once. Please think aloud while you answer this: how many windows are there in the house or apartment where you live?"

Let them answer, thinking aloud. If they only give the number, say: *"Good. This time try to say everything that goes through your head, even small things. How many doors are there?"* Move on once they're talking naturally.

## 3. During the practice and the worlds

- If they're silent for about **15 seconds**, say only: **"Please keep talking."** Nothing else: not "what are you thinking?", and no hints.
- Don't answer questions about the task during a world. Say: *"Do whatever you think is best. We can talk about it afterwards."*
- Note each reminder on the observation sheet. If they need reminders constantly, note that too.
- In the practice area you can give feedback: *"That's great, just like that."*

## 4. Recording

- Audio is recorded from the start of the practice to the end of the House, with the screen recording, so speech can be matched to what they were doing.
- Check the audio level during the practice: they should be clearly audible over the PC fans.
- File name: `<code>_Manual_thinkaloud.m4a`, for example `P04_Manual_thinkaloud.m4a`.

## 5. Transcribing

- Transcribe word for word, with time stamps every 30 s, or at the start of each task.
- Mark pauses of 3 s or more as `[pause]`, and inaudible parts as `[inaudible]`.
- Code the transcripts with the same scheme as the ROVR voice-command transcripts: route planning, landmark references, distances and directions, hesitation, error recovery. Then compare the two groups (§7.1.5).
